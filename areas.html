Where we work

What we find, where.

English building stock is regional. So is the way it fails.

London & the South East

Victorian terraces and converted basements

Solid stock-brick walls, tanked basement conversions with failed membranes, sunken lightwells holding water against the wall. Subsidence-linked cracking on clay soil is often confused with damp, and the two need telling apart before anyone specifies a fix.

Manchester & the North West

Millstone grit terraces and cellars

Dense gritstone and brick back-to-backs with unlined cellars prone to rising damp. High rainfall drives penetrating damp through solid walls, and corroded wall ties are a recurring finding in interwar cavity semis.

Birmingham & the Midlands

Mixed brick stock, cavity and solid

Victorian solid-wall terraces alongside 1930s cavity semis. Bridged cavities from a raised patio or render, blocked weep holes, and flat-roof extensions prone to penetrating damp at the junction with the main wall.

Yorkshire & the North East

Stone terraces, exposed hillside villages

Yorkshire stone and sandstone terraces on exposed hillside positions, taking driven rain on the weather gable. Failed pointing on exposed elevations and condensation in poorly ventilated loft conversions are the recurring finds.

Bristol, the South West & Cotswolds

Cotswold stone and lime-built walls

Soft limestone walls built to breathe, routinely sealed by cement render and modern repointing that traps moisture in instead of out. High exposure on coastal Devon and Cornwall properties, and damp at the eaves where a thatched roof meets the wall head.

East Anglia & the East

Flint, clay lump and timber frame

Flint and clay-lump construction with moisture behaviour that doesn't match a standard brick wall. Timber-framed buildings with wattle-and-daub infill prone to damp at sill level, and Fenland's high water table affecting sub-floor ventilation.
